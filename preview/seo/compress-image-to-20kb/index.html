When 20 KB is the ceiling

Some upload boxes refuse a photograph the moment it passes a small limit, even by a fraction of a kilobyte. Twenty is that kind of ceiling. The compressor on this page is aimed at it from the start, so you do not have to type the number.

A large camera file is shortened on its longest side to about 1,200 pixels before the quality search begins. That order keeps a face more readable than crushing a 4,000 pixel frame with quality alone. A picture that is already smaller is left at its own size. Nothing is stretched upward.

The result line prints the real size, for example 19.4 KB of 20 KB. The downloaded name uses that same figure, such as portrait-19.4kb.jpg. Batch works here too: compress the first set, add more, then compress only the new files and download a ZIP.

Change the number if the form you are filing asks for something else. A Navy registration page should be read on joinpaknavy.gov.pk before you treat 20 KB as the current rule. Stricter exam photos are covered on the FPSC and PPSC pages.

Questions

Is the file allowed to be exactly 20 KB?

Yes. The check is at or under the target, using 1,000 bytes per KB unless you switch to 1,024.

Will a photo that is already 15 KB be copied unchanged?

No. It is drawn again as a JPEG so camera metadata is not kept, and the new file still has to stay under 20 KB.

Why does a huge photo look soft at 20 KB?

There are not many bytes left for detail. The long side is capped first so the softness is spread more evenly.
